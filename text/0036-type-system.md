# RFC 0036: Type System

Pull request: [sic-lang/rfcs#36](https://github.com/sic-lang/rfcs/pull/36)

## Summary

This RFC states the type system of Sic as a whole: what it is made of, why each piece is there, what was set aside and why, and what the whole can and cannot do.
It gives no definition, rule or property itself: the system is defined in the annexes, the files of the directory that bears the name of this file, each on one subject and each built on the ones before it.

## Motivation

The type system of Sic combines dependent types with grades, which count uses.
Its pieces are tightly bound: the declared types need an equality to state their indices and their extensionality, and the equality is defined on the declared types, so that the system cannot be split into documents that each stand alone without one of them depending on a later one.
This RFC therefore states the system in one place, and keeps apart the two things a reader looks for: the main document says what the system is made of and why, for a reader who decides or reviews the design, and the annexes say exactly what it is, for a reader who checks or implements it.

## Proposal

The type system is presented in the annexes, in the order in which they build on each other.
This document lists the pieces of the system and the reasons for each of them, and refers to the annex that defines it.

### Grades

The type system counts uses, a use being an access to a value through a variable, along the two axes of the [grades](https://github.com/sic-lang/rfcs/pull/16) of Sic: the uses at run time, and all the uses, erased or not.
A [multiplicity](0036-type-system/03-multiplicities.md) counts the uses on one axis, as the multiplicities $`0`$, $`1`$ and $`\omega`$ of [Idris 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.00480) and of [Linear Haskell](https://ghc.gitlab.haskell.org/ghc/doc/users_guide/exts/linear_types.html) do, and a [grade](0036-type-system/04-grades.md) is a pair of multiplicities, one per axis.
The multiplicities and the grades each form a [modality](0036-type-system/02-modalities.md) of the graded type theory of [Abel, Danielsson and Eriksson](https://doi.org/10.1145/3607862), whose rules only know a modality by its laws, so that the rules of the type system follow theirs.
Their meaning is an abstract interpretation of the numbers of uses: each grade stands for the numbers of uses it allows, and its operations and its order are derived from those of the numbers rather than chosen.

The usage and the ownership of a term are checked in one of three [modes](0036-type-system/05-modes.md): no use, all the uses but none at run time, and all the uses.
The theory of Abel, Danielsson and Eriksson has only the first and the last; the mode in between is added so that an erased term, such as a proof, still counts all its uses, and cannot drop or duplicate a permission that may be used once.

### Levels and Sorts

The types live in [sorts](0036-type-system/07-sorts.md), the universes $`U_l`$, whose elements are types, and the universes of propositions $`\Omega_l`$, whose elements are propositions, both indexed by a [level](0036-type-system/06-levels.md).
A level is built from $`0`$, the successor, the larger of two levels and level variables, which stand for any natural numbers, so that an entry of the signature is checked once with its level variables and used at any levels, as in [Lean](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/The-Type-System/Universes/).
Two levels are equal or ordered when their values are, whatever numbers their variables take: the values are the meaning of the levels, and the equality and the order are exact for it by construction.

The quality of a sort, $`U`$ or $`\Omega`$, may be a sort variable, as the sort qualities of [Rocq](https://rocq-prover.org/doc/master/refman/addendum/universe-polymorphism.html), so that one entry of the signature serves for the types and for the propositions.
A sort bounds another when its level is above and it holds propositions only if the other does, and the join of two qualities gives the least sort that bounds two sorts at the same level.

### Syntax and Substitution

The [syntax](0036-type-system/08-syntax.md) has few terms: variables applied to arguments, sorts, instances of the constants of the signature, defined or declared, instances of the declared types, the constructors and the recursors of the data types, and the observations and the corecursors of the codata types, so that every type other than a sort comes from a type declaration, and its terms from the constructors and the recursor of its block, or from the corecursor of its block and its destructors.
A parameter of a type declaration may be a second-order variable, which stands for a term under the variables of a telescope, as the type of the result of a function depends on its argument.
The [substitution](0036-type-system/09-substitution.md) of the first order gives terms to the variables, and the substitution of the second order gives abstractions to the variables, as the arguments of an instance do to its parameters.

### Judgments, Usage and Ownership

The [typing](0036-type-system/14-typing.md) has the judgments of Martin-Löf type theory, a type, two equal types, a term of a type and two equal terms, in a signature and a context, and carries no grade; the [usage](0036-type-system/12-usage.md) is a judgment of its own, which checks in a mode how a term uses each variable of its scope, as a [vector of grades](0036-type-system/10-grade-vectors.md) allows, as in the theory of Abel, Danielsson and Eriksson.
The [ownership](0036-type-system/13-ownership.md) is a third judgment, which checks in a mode the ownership of the value of a term: a grade that counts the uses of this value during a whole run, through all the variables that lead to it.
Where the usage of a variable comes from the term that uses it, its ownership comes from the argument that its binder receives: each entry of a telescope requires an ownership of its argument, and a second-order variable states the ownership of its result from those of its arguments, as the meet of a constant and of factors, each scaling the ownership of one argument, so that a result may be a fresh value, an alias of an argument, or either.
A variable that a recursor or a corecursor binds has the ownership of the value it receives times the grade with which its scope uses it, so that a value received once and used twice through its variable is shared.

A program is a signature and a term, well typed, well used and well owned.
Its typing tells what it computes.
Its usage tells how it uses its resources, so that it states which resources it consumes, which proofs and specifications it erases, and which permissions it passes on exactly once.
Its ownership tells how its values are shared, so that it states which values no other variable reaches.
The ownership only reads grades of the syntax: those of the telescopes, which give the mode in which each argument is checked, as for the usage, and, with those of the recursive results of the branches, the uses of each variable that a recursor or a corecursor binds.
The three judgments are separate: no rule of one has a premise of another, for the terms as for the signature, which each checks with a judgment of its own, so that each judgment says what it checks, and nothing that another checks.

The signature lists the constant definitions, the constant declarations and the type declarations, in order, each checked against the entries before it, once with its level variables and for each instance of its sort and grade variables.
A constant has a mode, which says where it may be used: a constant of the mode $`{11}^{\mathsf{m}}`$ is a program, used anywhere, one of the mode $`{01}^{\mathsf{m}}`$ a proof or a ghost term, used in proofs and specifications, and one of the mode $`{00}^{\mathsf{m}}`$ a specification, used in specifications only.
A constant also has the ownership of its value, in every mode.
A constant definition is transparent: it unfolds to its body, whose ownership is checked against the one of the definition.
A constant declaration has no body, and covers three needs, told apart by the sort of its type and its mode: an axiom, a proposition assumed true, of the mode $`{01}^{\mathsf{m}}`$; a ghost constant, a value or a type that the specifications and the proofs use, of the mode $`{00}^{\mathsf{m}}`$ or $`{01}^{\mathsf{m}}`$; and a foreign constant, a type, a value or a function that the running program gets from outside, of the mode $`{11}^{\mathsf{m}}`$.
Its ownership is trusted, as its type is, so that a foreign constant states whether its value may be shared.
A type declaration declares a block of data types or a block of codata types, never both, each with the grades with which it uses the parameters of the block, which the elaboration computes and the type system checks against the uses of the fields of its constructors, all added; an instance uses its arguments as these grades say.
A type declaration has a constraint on the instances of its sort and grade variables, which gives those at which its types exist: the block is checked, and its types used, at these instances only, and the elaboration may infer the constraint as the instances at which the block is well formed.
The codata types have their syntax and their formation, a destructor being a second-order variable for the destructors after it, so that a destructor may depend on the observations before it, an observation carrying the instance of its term, as the recursor does, and a corecursor its own, as a constructor does.
A block of codata types is formed as a block of data types is, a destructor counting as one entry of a telescope, whose arguments live in the universe of the level of the block, whatever its quality, and whose result lives in a sort that the sort of the block bounds, or is a type of the block at the parameters themselves, the destructor being then recursive and no destructor after it depending on it; it enters the signature as a block of data types does, and its instances are types of its sort.
An observation of a term by a destructor, at arguments of its telescope, is a term of its result, the destructors before it being the observations of the term by them; it steps on a corecursor to the branch of the destructor, whatever instance it carries, uses its term once and its arguments as their grades at the instance say, in a mode that the grade of the destructor allows, and has the ownership of its term times that of the result of the destructor.
Two terms of an instance of a type without recursive destructor, whose arguments are arguments of the parameters, whose observations by each destructor are equal are equal, whatever the number of its destructors.
A corecursor from arguments of the state of its type is a term of the instance it carries, each branch being a term of the result of its destructor over the variables of the state and of the telescope, the destructors before it being the observations of the corecursor from the variables of the state, or a position, a new state or a term already built, if its destructor is recursive; two corecursors from equal states, with equal states, which are equal telescopes, and equal branches, are equal, an observation of a corecursor is equal to the branch of its destructor at the state and the arguments, resumed if the destructor is recursive, and a corecursor uses its state as many times as its grade says and the variables of its branches as a lower bound of their uses allows, each counted as many times as the grade of its destructor, and of the uses of a recursive branch plus its own, which the resumption repeats, builds a new value, and gives each branch the ownership of the result of its destructor at all the ownerships of its arguments, or that of a new value to a recursive branch.
The formation of a block of data types is given, for a block whose types occur in its fields only as the types of recursive fields, each at the parameters themselves and with no field after it depending on it, with its entry into the signature, its meaning and that of its instances, and its constructors, which build the terms of its instances: a constructor carries its instance, uses the variables of its fields as the arguments of a telescope do, and builds a new value, whose ownership states nothing of its fields, which the requirements of their entries state; its recursor carries the instance of the term it eliminates, whose arguments are arguments of the parameters, has a motive for each type of the block and a branch for each constructor, over the variables of its fields and of their recursive results, each the motive of the type of a recursive field at this field, steps on a constructor to its branch at the terms of its fields and at the recursors on those of its recursive fields, makes a term over a term of a type with a single constructor without recursive field equal to the recursor on this term whose branch is the term at the constructor, uses the term as its grade says, each field and its recursive result together as the grade of the field at the instance times that of the term says, and the variables of the scope as a bound of the uses of each branch and of the recursions of its recursive results, and gives each field the ownership of the term times the requirement of its entry, and each recursive result the ownership of the recursor.

The universes of propositions $`\Omega_l`$ hold strict propositions: two proofs of a proposition are equal, and an entry of a telescope whose type is a proposition has a grade without use at run time, so that the proofs are erased.

The [prelude](0036-type-system/19-prelude.md) is three type declarations that the type system uses for its own needs, and that the surface language never names: a unit, a pair, whose second field depends on the first, and a type of positions, which holds either a new state or a term already built, which the branch of a recursive destructor of a corecursor gives back, and whose resumption, a recursor, unpacks a new state into the corecursor from it.
A telescope packs into nested pairs that end with the unit, in a universe whatever the sorts of its entries, and its arguments into nested constructors of pairs; the declarations of the prelude are well formed, the packing of a telescope is a type, and the packing of arguments is a term of it, uses their variables as the arguments do and is a new value.
A term of the packing unpacks, by a recursor on each pair and then on the unit, into a term over the variables of the telescope, which the unpacking of a packing of arguments computes at the arguments, and every term over a term of the packing is the unpacking of this term.

### Reduction and Erasure

The [reduction](0036-type-system/15-reduction.md) is the computation of the type theory: one step of weak head reduction, untyped and defined on the terms of every scope, which unfolds a constant definition to its body at its instance and applies the computations that the kinds of type declarations give to their terms.
A closed term computes to a head normal form and stops there, without reducing its parts: the computation is lazy, as the meaning explanations of Martin-Löf type theory that [Nordström, Petersson and Smith](https://intuitionistic.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2010/07/nordstrom-et-al-type-theory.pdf) present, and tells what a term is, not how a program runs, whose evaluation order a later step of this RFC fixes, with the calling convention.

The [erasure](0036-type-system/16-erasure.md) compiles a term to an untyped target language, and replaces with a value $`\text{↯}`$ the parts whose grade is in a set of grades.
The set of the grades that a mode does not count gives, for the mode of run time, the program that runs, without its proofs and its specifications, as the extraction of Abel, Danielsson and Eriksson, and, for the mode of the proofs, the program that the proofs see, with its ghost code.
A variable whose grade is in the set is absent from the erasure of a term whose usage is checked in a mode that counts no use of the set, so that the usage states what the erasure removes; the erasure follows the grades alone, and depends on no evaluation order.
A constant keeps the grades of its instance, and the target language has one copy of it for each instance of its grades, so that no grade remains at run time.

### Meaning

The [meaning](0036-type-system/18-meaning.md) of the judgments follows the meaning explanations of Martin-Löf type theory: a closed judgment is true when its terms compute as it says, a judgment in a context when it is true under every pair of equal closed substitutions of the context, and a judgment with level variables when it is true at every value of its level variables.
Every judgment of the typing has a meaning, with the same objects, from the signatures to the arguments of an instance, and the meaning ignores the modes and the ownerships, as the typing does, and reads the grades only through the relations of run time of the types.
A closed canonical type is known by the equality of its canonical terms, which the sorts, the data types and the codata types give: a universe $`U_n`$ gives its types, each with the equality of its terms, a universe of propositions $`\Omega_n`$ its propositions, each with its truth, the closed terms of a true proposition being all equal proofs of it, an instance of a data type has its constructors as canonical terms, equal when they are the same constructor with equal fields, the equality of the instances of a block being the least relation closed under these clauses, in which the recursive fields are equal by the relation itself, a proposition declared as a data type being true when one of its constructors has terms for its fields, and an instance of a codata type has its corecursors as canonical terms, equal when their observations at equal arguments are equal, the equality of the instances of a block being the greatest relation consistent with these clauses, in which the results of the recursive destructors are equal by the relation itself, a proposition declared as a codata type being true when each of its destructors has a result at all its arguments.
Two types are equal when they have the same equality of terms and, in each program, the same relations of run time.
Each entry of a signature is meaningful in the whole signature, since a type declaration quantifies over the closed terms of its fields, which a larger signature may add.
A constant declaration has no body to compute: the meaning takes an interpretation, which gives each constant declaration a term, and computes in the signature where the declarations become definitions with these terms.
The typing is sound: under an interpretation that gives each constant declaration a term of its type, every judgment that the rules derive is true, so that the axioms and the foreign constants of a program are the hypotheses of its guarantees.

What the usage and the ownership of a term guarantee of its erasure when it runs, and the soundness of the erasure, are not stated yet: they need the evaluation order that a later step of this RFC fixes, with the calling convention.

## Drawbacks

The type system has these drawbacks:

- One RFC for the whole type system is a large document to review, even with the definitions in the annexes; its parts are accepted together, since none of them stands alone.
- Seven grades, among which a programmer chooses on every binder, are many: the surface language has to infer most of them, which no RFC designs yet.
- The ownership adds a requirement to every entry of a telescope and an ownership to every second-order variable and constant, which the surface language has to infer as well.
- Without cumulativity, a program polymorphic in its universes writes its levels and their joins, as in Agda and in Lean.
- Without impredicative propositions, the encodings that quantify a proposition over all propositions, which Lean and Rocq accept, are not available.
- The constant definitions are transparent: a library cannot hide the body of a definition from the equality, until the modules of a later RFC give a way to hide it.
- A constant whose body checks a position with a grade variable is compiled once for each instance of its grades that a program uses, which enlarges the program, as the monomorphization of Rust does.
- The soundness holds under an interpretation of the constant declarations: a signature whose axioms contradict each other has none, so that its programs get no guarantee, and the type system does not check that the axioms of a program have an interpretation.
- The meaning of the grades counts the uses that a term makes, not those that a program makes when it runs, which depend on the evaluation order; the run-time multiplicities $`1`$ and $`\omega`$ wait for the calling convention, which a later step of this RFC fixes, to mean a number of uses.

## Rationale and Alternatives

This section gives the principles of the design, then the choices that settle each part of the system, in the order of the proposal, and why the system is one RFC.

### Principles

The design of the type system follows a few principles, which explain most of its choices:

- Soundness first: a program the compiler accepts has the properties its types state, as [RFC 13](https://github.com/sic-lang/rfcs/pull/13) requires, and no other benefit justifies weakening it.
- No two mechanisms for the same thing: a need that an existing piece already meets gets no second piece, so that every piece has one role and the system stays small.
- Few pieces, but powerful ones: a piece is chosen general enough to cover several needs, as a codata type whose destructors take arguments gives both the functions and the additive conjunction $`\&`$ of linear logic.
- Embrace duality where it exists: when a construct has a dual, both are given together and named after each other, as the data types and the codata types.
- No speculative mechanism: a piece enters the system for a use that exists, not for one that might come.
- Conscious exclusions: what the system leaves out is stated with what is lost, what remains, and whether it can be added later.

### Choices on the Grades

The grades are settled by the following choices:

- The grades are pairs of multiplicities, one for each axis, rather than a flat set of named grades: the operations and the order act on each component, which gives their tables and their properties at once, and the names of the grades in Sic are read off the components.
- The order is the meet, as in the theory of Abel, Danielsson and Eriksson, rather than the reverse inclusion of the abstract interpretation turned into an order of its own: their rules use the meet as the order, so that the grades instantiate them as they are; the two orders coincide.
- The meet is the best abstraction of the union of the uses, so that a variable used differently by two branches gets the grade that allows both, rather than the join, which would allow fewer uses than either branch makes.
- The grade $`{\omega 1}^{\mathsf{p}}`$, `affine / linear`, counts exactly one use in total, made at run time or erased, as a permission that one branch passes on to the code and another to a proof; it is the meet of $`{01}^{\mathsf{p}}`$ and $`{11}^{\mathsf{p}}`$, and without it, this meet would be $`{\omega\omega}^{\mathsf{p}}`$, and the multiplication would not distribute over the meet, as a modality requires, since $`{{01}^{\mathsf{p}} \cdot {\omega\omega}^{\mathsf{p}}}`$ is $`{0\omega}^{\mathsf{p}}`$ while $`{({01}^{\mathsf{p}} \cdot {01}^{\mathsf{p}}) \wedge ({01}^{\mathsf{p}} \cdot {11}^{\mathsf{p}})}`$ is $`{01}^{\mathsf{p}}`$.
- The pairs $`{\langle 1, 0 \rangle}`$ and $`{\langle \omega, 0 \rangle}`$ are left out rather than identified with $`{00}^{\mathsf{p}}`$: they have no meaning of their own, and keeping them would break the well-behaved zero that the modes require, since $`{\langle 1, 0 \rangle \cdot {01}^{\mathsf{p}}}`$ and $`{\langle \omega, 0 \rangle \cdot {01}^{\mathsf{p}}}`$ are both $`{00}^{\mathsf{p}}`$.
- The properties of the greatest lower bounds hold for arbitrary families, since a family of grades takes at most seven values, rather than being assumed, as [Eriksson, Abel and Danielsson](https://doi.org/10.1145/3828677) assume them for a modality that may be infinite.
- Conscious exclusions, each with what is lost, what remains, and whether it can be added later:
  - Exact counts above one, such as a grade for exactly two uses: a resource used twice is unrestricted; the pairs of multiplicities would extend to pairs of larger modalities, as the [exact usage](https://doi.org/10.1145/3341714) of Granule, at the cost of larger tables and of the finiteness of the grades.
  - A greatest grade that allows no number of uses, the best abstraction of the empty set: no operation needs it, and no multiplicity can be added above $`0`$, $`1`$ and $`\omega`$ without breaking the distributivity, since $`{(0 \wedge 1) \cdot \varnothing = (0 \cdot \varnothing) \wedge (1 \cdot \varnothing)}`$ would give $`{\omega \cdot \varnothing = 0}`$, hence $`{\varnothing + \varnothing = 0}`$ since $`{\omega = 1 + 1}`$, and $`{\varnothing = 0}`$ by the monotonicity of the addition.

### Choices on the Levels and Sorts

The levels and the sorts are settled by the following choices:

- The levels are not terms: they appear only as the indices of the sorts, and their equality is defined by their values rather than by rules of equality, unlike the formalization of Abel, Danielsson and Eriksson, whose levels are terms of a type of levels since [Danielsson, Favier and Kubánek](https://doi.org/10.1145/3776645); no term computes a level, so that no rule is needed to compare two of them, and their equality is decidable.
- The sort of a type declaration may be the join of two qualities, as its level may be the join of two levels, so that a pair holds two fields of independent sorts; the sort constraints of the [bounded sort polymorphism](https://doi.org/10.1145/3776732) of Rocq would be one mechanism more, for this need only.
- Conscious exclusion of the quantification over levels: no type quantifies over levels, so that no universe is needed beyond the two hierarchies, as the $`\mathsf{Set}\omega`$ of [Agda](https://agda.readthedocs.io/en/latest/language/universe-levels.html) is; the level variables give the polymorphism that programs need, and a quantification could be added later with a universe above the hierarchies.

### Choices on the Syntax and Substitution

The syntax and the substitution are settled by the following choices:

- The variables are named, as in the paper of Abel, Danielsson and Eriksson, rather than numbered by the de Bruijn indices of its formalization: an index is a way to represent the bound variables, which the system does not need to fix.
- The sort and grade variables of an entry are not part of the terms: an entry stands for its instances, as a rule for the instances of its fraction, so that no judgment meets a grade variable, whose order with the other grades would be unknown; the level variables are part of the terms, since an entry is checked once with them and used at any levels.
- A type declaration has a constraint, any predicate on the instances of its sort and grade variables, rather than being checked at all of them: a field of a proposition has a grade without use at run time, so that a pair whose first field has a grade variable is a proposition only at the instances where this grade is without use, and checking every instance would forbid it; a constraint that admits no instance is rejected, since the block would declare nothing.
- The constraint is any predicate, rather than a formula of a language of constraints, as the [elimination constraints](https://rocq-prover.org/doc/master/refman/addendum/universe-polymorphism.html#elimination-of-sort-polymorphic-inductives) of the sort polymorphism of Rocq are: Rocq checks a polymorphic entry once, with its variables, while an entry of Sic is checked at each of its instances, which are finitely many, so that the elaboration may infer the constraint as the instances at which the block is well formed, as the kernel of Lean 4 [computes](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/blob/5045d0056413266e57c625dcd7c365b10e377c52/src/kernel/inductive.cpp#L522) rather than reads the eliminations that an inductive proposition allows, and reject a block one of whose variables takes a value at no instance it admits, a variable that does not vary.
- The prelude is fixed once, three declarations that the type system holds, rather than derived declarations declared where they are needed: a declaration is generative, so that two packings of one telescope would be two types unless a rule identified them; its names are reserved, and the elaboration renames a declaration of a program that has one of them.
- The surface language never names the prelude: its declarations serve the rules, and may change with them, while a name of the surface language is a commitment to its users.
- The packing of a telescope lives in a universe, whatever the sorts of its entries, rather than in the sort of the telescope: a packing in a universe of propositions would be a proposition, whose recursor builds only proofs, so that a telescope of proofs could not be unpacked into a term of a universe; the cost is a value at run time made of erased fields, and the product `PProd` of Lean 4, which [lives](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/blob/5045d0056413266e57c625dcd7c365b10e377c52/src/Init/Prelude.lean#L572) in `Sort (max 1 u v)` whatever the sorts of its fields, makes the same choice; the type of positions does likewise, and the grade of its term already built is a variable, which is without use at run time when this term is a proof.
- The second parameter of the pair, a type over the first field, uses this field with the grade $`{\omega\omega}^{\mathsf{p}}`$, which states nothing, rather than with a grade variable: the parameters of a type declaration are the same at all its instances, so that the grades of the telescope of a second-order parameter cannot vary with them, and a packing, which is a type, needs no grade there.
- The second field of the pair requires the ownership $`{11}^{\mathsf{p}}`$ of a new value, which every packing of arguments has, rather than nothing: a recursor gives each field the ownership of the term it eliminates times the requirement of the field, so that, with the requirement $`{\omega\omega}^{\mathsf{p}}`$, every entry after the first would be shared once unpacked.
- The new state of a position requires the ownership $`{11}^{\mathsf{p}}`$ of a new value, as the second field of the pair does, rather than nothing, so that its unpacking keeps the ownership of each entry of the state; the term already built of a position requires the ownership that a grade variable gives, rather than nothing, so that the type of the positions of a destructor states the ownership of its result.
- The parameters that depend on variables are second-order variables, as the contextual types of Nanevski, Pfenning and Pientka, rather than parameters of a type of functions: the type of functions is itself declared, and its type declaration cannot take a function as a parameter.
- The substitutions of the first and of the second order are defined apart, as in the theory of Nanevski, Pfenning and Pientka: the second one substitutes the arguments of an application in the abstraction it gives, which is not a part of the term it substitutes, and the first one does it.
- A renaming may rename two variables to the same one, rather than keep them apart and in order, as the weakenings of the formalization of Abel, Danielsson and Eriksson do: the usage adds the uses of the variables renamed to the same one, the ownership asks them the same ownership, and the typing the same type, so that the judgments hold under every renaming that their premises allow.

### Choices on the Judgments, Usage and Ownership

The judgments, the usage and the ownership are settled by the following choices:

- Typing and equality carry no grade, as in the theory of Abel, Danielsson and Eriksson, rather than carrying them in the typing judgment, as the [graded modal dependent type theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.13163) of Moon, Eades and Orchard does: the latter checks every subterm as if it ran, even in an erased position, so that a specification cannot return an erased field; a usage indexed by a mode checks an erased subterm in a mode of its own, and the equality compares values, not uses, which removes no guarantee, since the usage of every term is checked.
- The judgments are the four of Martin-Löf type theory, as in the theory of Abel, Danielsson and Eriksson and in [Abel, Öhman and Vezzosi](https://doi.org/10.1145/3158111), rather than those of a [pure type system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pure_type_system), in which a type is a term of a sort and two types are equal only as terms of a sort, so that two equalities of types in different sorts compose only once the two sorts are shown equal; a type is still a term of a sort, as the only rules that conclude that a term is a type say.
- The signature is explicit in every judgment, as the global environment of [Rocq](https://rocq-prover.org/doc/master/refman/language/cic.html), rather than ambient: a rule says which entries it reads.
- A constant definition is an entry of the signature with a mode, rather than a binding in a term or a text to inline: it is checked once, with its sort, level and grade variables, and used in the modes it allows, as the modes of [Verus](https://doi.org/10.1145/3586037) separate the specifications, the proofs and the code that runs; inlining would check each use again, and lose the polymorphism of its variables.
- The constant definitions are transparent: an opaque definition would hide a body from the equality, which the strict propositions already do for the proofs, and which the modules of a later RFC may do for the other terms.
- One entry, the constant declaration, covers the axioms, the ghost constants and the foreign constants, told apart by their sort and their mode, rather than three entries, or primitive types with rules of their own, as the [primitive integers and arrays](https://rocq-prover.org/doc/master/refman/language/core/primitive.html) of Rocq: a foreign type is a constant whose representation the implementation chooses.
  A constant uses no variable, so that a foreign function that creates a resource to be used once, such as a handle, must consume a resource to do so, a capability that the program holds with a linear grade, as the world token of [Idris 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.00480) threads its effects.
- The propositions are strict, as the `Prop` of [Agda](https://agda.readthedocs.io/en/latest/language/prop.html), from [Gilbert, Cockx, Sozeau and Tabareau](https://doi.org/10.1145/3290316): a proof carries no information but its existence, so that two proofs of a proposition are equal by definition, and a proof is never needed at run time.
- The ownership is a judgment of its own, rather than an attribute of the types, as the unique types of [Marshall, Vollmer and Orchard](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-99336-8_13) and the uniqueness mode of [Lorenzen, White, Dolan, Eisenberg and Lindley](https://doi.org/10.1145/3674642): the typing and the equality carry no grade, as for the usage, and the ownership counts with the grades of the usage, so that it adds no structure on the grades.
- The ownership counts the uses of a value during a whole run, rather than its past uses only, as uniqueness does, or its future uses only, as linearity does, which Marshall, Vollmer and Orchard tell apart: one grade states both, and the judgment follows no order of the uses.
  A program passes a value from one use to the next, as a read that returns the value it reads, so that the order of the uses is that of the data, and the ownership depends on no order of evaluation, which a later step of this RFC fixes; a value used before a use that requires it alone, without being passed from one to the other, is rejected, although the earlier use is over.
- The ownership of the variables is the same in every premise of a rule, as the context of the typing, rather than computed by each premise and added, as the vector of the usage: the ownership of a variable comes from the argument that its binder receives, while its usage comes from the term that uses it.
- Each entry of a telescope has a requirement of its own, rather than the telescope one relation on the ownerships of all its arguments, so that each argument is checked alone, as the usage checks it with the grade of its entry.
- The ownership of the result of a second-order variable is a meet of an optional constant and of optional factors, rather than one grade or one argument whose ownership it takes: a result may be an alias of an argument in one branch and a fresh value in another, and the coefficient of a factor scales the ownership of the argument, as for an alias that a proof also mentions.
- The ownership of a constant declaration is trusted, as its type is, rather than left out: a foreign constant has no body to check, and its ownership says whether its value may be shared, which no other piece states.
- A constructor gives its value the ownership $`{11}^{\mathsf{p}}`$ of a new value, rather than the meet of the ownerships of its fields scaled by coefficients, as for the result of a second-order variable: the ownership of a value says whether its cell is shared, and its type says what the value holds, by the requirements of the entries of the fields, which grade variables make vary from one instance to another, so that a list of shared values still has cells owned alone, which a program may reuse in place, as [Perceus](https://doi.org/10.1145/3453483.3454032) reuses the cells that it frees; a meet would state the ownership of the cell and of what it holds in one grade, and a value with a shared field would lose the ownership of its cell.
- The recursor carries the instance of the term it eliminates, as a constructor carries its own, rather than leaving it to the type of the term: the usage and the ownership are defined on the term and the signature alone, and read the grades and the requirements of the fields at this instance, which grade variables make vary from one instance to another.
- The motives on a block of propositions that has a constructor are propositions, rather than any type when each type of the block has one constructor at most: the proofs of a proposition need not compute, so that a recursor on a proof, whose motive is not a proposition, would have no constructor to step on; on a block without constructor, no closed proof exists, and the motives may be any type.
- A field of a branch has the ownership of the term times the requirement of its entry, rather than its requirement alone, which would let a program take a value owned alone out of a shared one twice, or the least ownership, which would forbid the reuse in place of the fields: [Perceus](https://doi.org/10.1145/3453483.3454032) likewise takes over the fields of a cell owned alone, and shares those of a shared cell.
- A recursor in the mode $`{11}^{\mathsf{m}}`$ uses its term at run time only when a type of the block has two constructors or more, rather than always: with one constructor, the branch to run is known, and the branch uses the fields only as $`{\ulcorner m \urcorner \cdot r}`$ times their grades allow, which has no use at run time when $`r`$ has none; with none, no closed term of the type exists.
- The erasure of a recursor whose term it removes runs the recursor on the skeleton of the type of the term, the form that the type gives to its closed terms, rather than running the branch on erased fields and results, or forbidding the use of the recursive results at run time: a branch may use a recursive result at run time even when the term has no use, since the result is a computation of the recursor, whose uses the branches pay, and not a part of the term, so that erasing it would change the value of the program, and forbidding it would reject programs that are right.
- A type with a single constructor has the extensionality $`(\eta_c)`$, and the types with none or with two or more have none, rather than all the types or none: two terms of a type with a single constructor are compared by their fields without guessing anything, while a type with no constructor or with several would have the comparison guess a split into cases, which [Agda](https://agda.readthedocs.io/en/latest/language/record-types.html), [Lean](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/The-Type-System/Inductive-Types/#structures) and [Rocq](https://rocq-prover.org/doc/master/refman/language/core/records.html) likewise avoid by giving $`\eta`$ only to their records and structures.
- The extensionality $`(\eta_c)`$ equates any term over a term of the type to the recursor whose branch is this term at the constructor, rather than only the term to its constructor applied to its fields: without projections, the fields are reached only through a recursor, and the weaker form would not let a term over the term enter the branch, while the stronger one makes every recursor on such a type equal to its branch, so that none is stuck.
- The extensionality $`(\eta_c)`$ holds in a block of any number of types, with any motives and branches for the other types, rather than in a block of one type: the recursor on a term of one type runs only the branch of its constructor, so that grouping types in a block does not take their extensionality away.
- The arguments of a destructor live in the universe $`U_l`$ of the level $`l`$ of its block, rather than in the sort of the block as the fields of a constructor do: a block of propositions then quantifies over the types of its level, such as the natural numbers, as the propositions of [Agda](https://agda.readthedocs.io/en/latest/language/prop.html) do, which stay predicative, since the propositions of a level are a type of the level above it.
- A destructor counts the uses of its arguments and of its result as many times as its grade says, as a field counts those of its type, rather than once whatever its grade: the two agree on the functions of [Abel, Danielsson and Eriksson](https://github.com/graded-type-theory/graded-type-theory/blob/1f720ee636cb16aec130cc1ddc1c32acea8809d0/Graded/Usage.agda#L363), whose result is used once and whose domain counts its uses as the grade of the argument says, as the entries of the arguments of a destructor do, and only the first counts the destructors of a codata type as the fields of a data type are counted.
- The rule of an observation has the arguments of the instance as a premise, as the rule of a constructor has, rather than leaving them to the type of its term, of which they are arguments by inversion: the result of the destructor is a type at equal arguments of the parameters, which the destructors of a meaningful declaration give, while the meaning of the type of the term gives only the equality of its terms.
- An observation uses its term once, in its own mode, which the grade of the destructor must allow, rather than as many times as this grade: each observation runs one branch of a corecursor once, as an application uses its function once in the theory of [Abel, Danielsson and Eriksson](https://github.com/graded-type-theory/graded-type-theory/blob/1f720ee636cb16aec130cc1ddc1c32acea8809d0/Graded/Usage.agda), and the grade of the destructor says where its result exists, as the mode of a constant says where the constant may be used, so that a result without use at run time, such as a proof, is observed only in a term without use at run time.
- A recursor on a block of propositions that has a constructor is checked only in a mode other than $`{11}^{\mathsf{m}}`$, as an observation whose result is a proof, rather than in any mode, or erased whole by the erasure, which would read the sort of the block: its motives are then propositions, so that it builds a proof, which has no use at run time, and its term is a proof, which no value at run time stands for, so that running it would eliminate a value that the proof does not give; the erasure then follows the grades alone, and the eliminations of the empty propositions, whose motives may be types, stay in the code, where they are never reached.
- An observation has the ownership of its term times that of the result of its destructor, rather than that of the result alone: a corecursor may give back a value that it captured, so that the result of a shared value may be reached by an other owner of the value, as a field of a shared cell is shared, which the recursor states likewise.
- Each state of a corecursor carries the sorts of its entries, and is a telescope in these sorts below the join of their levels, rather than a telescope of one sort for all the states, of a sort that the sort of the block bounds, or of a sort of its own: the packing of a state, which the position of a recursive destructor holds, needs the sort of each entry, which a sort only bounds; the meaning of a corecursor is that of its observations, which do not read its state, so that no sort of the state threatens the consistency.
- The branch of a recursive destructor gives back a position, a new state, packed, or a term already built, rather than a state alone or a term alone: a state alone could never stop, and a term alone could never resume the corecursor, while both are needed to define, for instance, a stream that goes on from a state and ends with a stream already built; the observation resumes the position by a recursor of the prelude, rather than by a reduction rule of its own, so that the reduction and its meaning stay those of the recursors.
- The ownership of the result of a recursive destructor is a constant, rather than any ownership: the term already built of a position requires this constant, which a type of positions holds as an instance of its grade variable, while factors would make it depend on the ownerships of the arguments of the destructor, which no type states; and this constant is below the ownership $`{11}^{\mathsf{p}}`$ of a new value, rather than any constant: an observation that resumes the corecursor gives a new value, so that a constant without use at run time, $`{00}^{\mathsf{p}}`$, $`{01}^{\mathsf{p}}`$ or $`{0\omega}^{\mathsf{p}}`$, would state of it what it does not hold, and such a constant would accept as a term already built no value that a constructor or a corecursor builds.
- A corecursor bounds its uses by those of the branch of each recursive destructor plus its own, in addition to those of the branch alone, rather than by those of the branch alone: the observation resumes the position that the branch gives into the corecursor, which uses the variables of its branches again, or gives the term already built, which does not, so that a variable that a recursive branch uses is used as many times as the corecursor is observed, as the eliminator of the natural numbers of [Abel, Danielsson and Eriksson](https://github.com/graded-type-theory/graded-type-theory/blob/1f720ee636cb16aec130cc1ddc1c32acea8809d0/Graded/Usage.agda) bounds its uses by a fixed point, and a variable used once by the other branches is used at most once.
- The branch of a recursive destructor has the ownership $`{11}^{\mathsf{p}}`$ of a new value, rather than the ownership of the result of the destructor: the resumption is a recursor on the position, which gives the term already built the ownership of the position times the ownership $`\chi`$ that the position requires of it, so that this term keeps $`\chi`$ only in a position owned alone, which every position built by a constructor is.
- A corecursor whose block has a recursive destructor needs a signature that holds the prelude, rather than every term: the prelude serves only the positions, so that the other blocks are checked without it.
- In the type of a branch of a corecursor, the destructors before its destructor are the observations of the corecursor from the variables of the state, rather than the branches of these destructors: the type of the branch is then the type of the observation of the corecursor, by the same substitution of the observations, and the branches before it are equal to these observations by $`(\beta)`$, which a type that reads them reaches by conversion.
- A branch of a corecursor is checked in the mode of the grade of its destructor, its uses counted as many times as this grade, and the corecursor uses the variables as a lower bound of the uses of its branches allows, rather than checking all its branches in its own mode with the same uses, as a recursor does: the branch of a destructor without use at run time, such as a proof, may then use the variables that the run does not count, and the branches are alternatives, of which an observation runs one, as the additive conjunction of linear logic shares its context, while the fields of a constructor are stored together, whose uses add up.
- The erasure removes a branch of a corecursor whose destructor has a grade that it removes, as it removes an argument of such a grade, rather than keeping every branch: the branch is checked in the mode of this grade, which counts uses that the run does not count, so that the absence of a variable from the erasure needs it.
- The sets that erase only what a mode does not count erase every product of which they erase a factor, a condition that the sets of the modes satisfy: the state of a corecursor is erased at the grade of each entry times the grade of the corecursor, so that the absence of a variable from its erasure needs a product to be erased when the corecursor or the entry has no use.
- A variable that a recursor or a corecursor binds has the ownership of the value it receives times the grade with which its scope uses it, rather than the ownership of the value alone, which would let a branch pass a value owned alone twice where entries require it alone, which the count of the uses during a whole run rejects: the factor is the grade that the usage gives the variable, read from the syntax, so that the ownership stays independent of the derivations of the usage.
- A branch carries the grades of its recursive results, as the elimination of the natural numbers of [Abel, Danielsson and Eriksson](https://github.com/graded-type-theory/graded-type-theory/blob/1f720ee636cb16aec130cc1ddc1c32acea8809d0/Graded/Usage.agda) carries the grade of its recursive result, rather than leaving them to the derivation of the usage, which the ownership does not read, or bounding them by the grade of the recursive field: when no type of the block has two constructors, a recursor may have a grade without use at run time, and its branch use a recursive result at run time all the same, which the bound would give no use at run time.
- A branch of a corecursor has the ownership of the result of its destructor at all the ownerships of its arguments that satisfy their requirements, rather than at their requirements only: an observation reads this ownership at the ownerships of its arguments, which may be higher than their requirements, so that a branch that gives back a shared value, for a result whose ownership follows that of an argument, would be accepted at the requirement and observed as owned alone.
- Two corecursors are equal when their states are equal telescopes, rather than the same telescopes or any telescopes: a state equal up to conversion then gives an equal corecursor, as an equal motive gives an equal recursor; the meaning reads the states of the first corecursor only, as the contexts of its branches, so that the soundness does not need this premise.
- Two telescopes are equal when their entries have the same grades, rather than whatever their grades, as their meaning relates them: two types that differ only by a grade are different types, and the grades of a state say how the corecursor uses it.
- A codata type without recursive destructor has the extensionality $`(\eta_d)`$, whatever its number of destructors, rather than none: two terms are compared by their observations without guessing anything, so that a function is equal to its $`\eta`$-expansion, as a record of [Agda](https://agda.readthedocs.io/en/latest/language/record-types.html#eta-expansion) is equal to the record of its projections, and a type without destructors has all its terms equal, as $`\eta`$ makes a record without fields have a single term.
- The premises of $`(\eta_d)`$ read the destructors before each destructor as the observations of the first term, rather than of each term on its side: the observations of the two terms by these destructors are equal by the premises before, so that one context suffices, as the rule $`(d_\equiv)`$ reads the observations of its first term.
- A recursive field is a type of its block at the parameters themselves, on which no field after it depends, rather than any occurrence of a type of the block that the strict positivity allows: the meaning relates the recursive fields by any relation that the least relation contains, which the fields after one would have to respect if they depended on it, and a field that depended on a recursive field would mention the block in a term, an occurrence that the positivity excludes; the nested occurrences and the parameters that vary come with the liftings and the families of states.
- The recursor and the extensionality of the data types have the arguments of the instance as a premise, as the constructors do, rather than leaving them to the type of the term: the meaning of a recursive field of a branch is the one of the instance of the block at these arguments, which the soundness reads from the meaning of the parameters.
- A recursive destructor has a type of its block at the parameters themselves as its result, on which no destructor after it depends, as a recursive field, rather than any occurrence that the strict positivity allows: the meaning relates the results of the recursive destructors by any relation consistent with the clauses, which the destructors after one would have to respect if they depended on it; the extensionality of the codata types has the arguments of the instance as a premise, as that of the data types, since its soundness reads the instances of the results of the recursive destructors from the meaning of the parameters.
- The recursive results of a branch come after all its fields, rather than each after its field: no field depends on a recursive result, and the type of a result mentions its field only, so that the fields keep the telescope of the constructor and the reduction substitutes the terms of the fields, then the recursors on them, as a minor premise of a recursor of [Lean](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/The-Type-System/Inductive-Types/#recursor-types) takes the results of the recursion in addition to the fields of its constructor, after them.
- A recursive result is the recursor at the same instance, with the same motives and the same branches, on the term of its field, rather than a recursor of its own: the parameters are uniform and each recursive field is at the instance of the block, so that the recursion on it is the same elimination, whose type is the motive of the type of the field.
- The uses of a recursive field and of its recursive result together cover the grade of the field times that of the recursor, $`{\ulcorner m \urcorner \cdot r \cdot q \le g + e \cdot r}`$, rather than each the whole grade: a use of the result runs the recursor on the field, which uses it $`r`$ times, so that a branch may consume a linear field through its recursive result alone, as the recursion on the natural numbers of [Eriksson, Abel and Danielsson](https://doi.org/10.1145/3828677) allows.
- The recursor uses the variables of the scope as a bound $`\chi`$ of the uses of each branch plus those of the recursions of its recursive results, $`{\chi \le \delta + (e_1 + \dots + e_b) \cdot \chi}`$, rather than as the least uses of all the unfoldings of the recursion: the inequalities are what the step $`(\iota)`$ needs, each recursive result being a recursor that uses the scope as $`\chi`$, and checking them computes no fixed point, as the rule of the recursion on the natural numbers without $`\mathsf{nr}`$ of [Abel, Danielsson and Eriksson](https://github.com/graded-type-theory/graded-type-theory/blob/1f720ee636cb16aec130cc1ddc1c32acea8809d0/Graded/Usage.agda#L438) does.
- A recursive result has the ownership of the recursor times the grade that its branch gives it, rather than the least ownership: the step $`(\iota)`$ substitutes for it the recursor on the field, whose result has this ownership, and an evaluation may compute it once for all its uses.
- The extensionality $`(\eta_c)`$ holds for a single constructor without recursive field, rather than for any single constructor or only in a block without recursive field: on a recursive field, the expansion could go on without end, which [Agda](https://agda.readthedocs.io/en/latest/language/record-types.html#unguarded-records) prevents only when a type without $`\eta`$ guards the field, with a check that only warns, while a constructor without recursive field expands once, whatever the other types of its block, whereas [Lean](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/blob/5045d0056413266e57c625dcd7c365b10e377c52/src/kernel/inductive.cpp#L32) gives $`\eta`$ to no type of a block that [one of its constructors makes recursive](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/blob/5045d0056413266e57c625dcd7c365b10e377c52/src/kernel/inductive.cpp#L361).
- The extensionality $`(\eta_d)`$ holds for a codata type none of whose destructors is recursive, rather than for every codata type, although the meaning makes it hold for every one, or only in a block without recursive destructor: comparing two terms by their observations would go on without end on a recursive destructor, as on two variables of a type of streams, whose tails are compared again by their observations, which is why [Agda](https://agda.readthedocs.io/en/latest/language/coinduction.html) gives $`\eta`$ to no coinductive record unless a pragma forces it, since it "can make the type checker loop", while a type without recursive destructor compares each observation once, whatever the other types of its block, as $`(\eta_c)`$ expands a constructor without recursive field.
- The grades with which a declared type uses its parameters are declared and checked, rather than computed by the type system as the greatest that its constructors allow: the elaboration computes them, as it infers the grades of the other terms, and the type system only checks them, without the inference of the greatest usage of a term, which a recursive block would turn into a fixed point; [Agda](https://agda.readthedocs.io/en/latest/language/runtime-irrelevance.html#syntax) likewise has the erased arguments declared, the forcing analysis of its compiler being an optimization.
- The uses of the constructors of a type are added, rather than bounded constructor by constructor: a type mentions all its constructors, as the types of the functions and of the pairs of [Abel, Danielsson and Eriksson](https://github.com/graded-type-theory/graded-type-theory/blob/1f720ee636cb16aec130cc1ddc1c32acea8809d0/Graded/Usage.agda#L363) add the uses of their components, so that a parameter of the type of the optional values, used by one constructor only, is used once.
  Bounding each constructor alone would take the meet of their uses, as the binary sums that Abel, Danielsson and Eriksson [derive](https://github.com/graded-type-theory/graded-type-theory/blob/1f720ee636cb16aec130cc1ddc1c32acea8809d0/Graded/Derived/Sum.agda#L117) from the pairs and the booleans do, a meet that comes from the choice of a type by a case on a boolean, which a declared type does not make, and which gives $`{\omega\omega}^{\mathsf{p}}`$ for $`{{00}^{\mathsf{p}} \wedge {11}^{\mathsf{p}}}`$.
  A parameter used by several constructors is then counted several times, $`{{11}^{\mathsf{p}} + {11}^{\mathsf{p}} = {\omega\omega}^{\mathsf{p}}}`$; the instances are erased at run time, so that either choice only decides which programs are accepted.
- Conscious exclusions, each with what is lost, what remains, and whether it can be added later:
  - Impredicative propositions: a proposition cannot quantify over all propositions; the levels give the quantifications over the propositions of a lower level, which cover the usual encodings, and the sort variables give one entry for the types and the propositions.
    With definitional proof irrelevance, impredicativity makes the equality of types undecidable, as [Abel and Coquand](https://doi.org/10.23638/LMCS-16(2:14)2020) show, so it is excluded rather than deferred.
  - Cumulativity: a type of $`U_l`$ is not a type of $`{U_{l + 1}}`$; it is deferred, since adding it later only accepts more programs, at the cost of a subtyping in place of the equality of types.
  - Fractions of ownership: several uses of a value at once followed by a use that requires it alone, which the [fractional uniqueness](https://doi.org/10.1145/3649848) of Marshall and Orchard accepts, are rejected, since the sum $`{1 + 1 = \omega}`$ of the multiplicities cannot be undone; the sequential case passes the value from one use to the next, and fractions could be added later, at the cost of a larger modality.

### Choices on the Reduction and Erasure

The reduction and the erasure are settled by the following choices:

- The reduction is weak head, rather than to full normal forms: the meaning of a closed term and the comparison of two terms need only their heads, as in the decision procedure for the equality of [Abel, Öhman and Vezzosi](https://doi.org/10.1145/3158111), which the formalization of Abel, Danielsson and Eriksson extends; it is untyped, so that a step needs no typing, and that a step keeps the type of a term is a property of the reduction, not a condition of its steps.
- The reduction is the computation of the type theory, not the evaluation of a program: the computation is deterministic, and the guarantees of the system hold whatever the order in which a program runs.
  The costs, the order of the foreign effects and the evaluation of the parts of a term that are taken one at a time belong to the calling convention, which a later step of this RFC fixes, and whose direction is the call-by-push-value of [Levy](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-94-007-0954-6), by polarity, as [Downen, Sullivan, Ariola and Peyton Jones](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-17184-1_5) evaluate data by value and codata on observation; that step has to check again, on the machine it chooses, the resource correctness of the usage of recursion, which [Eriksson, Abel and Danielsson](https://doi.org/10.1145/3828677) prove for a call-by-name machine.
- The computation of an observation of a corecursor is named $`(\beta)`$, rather than after the projections or the observations: the functions are codata types, whose one destructor is the application, so that the $`\beta`$ rule of the λ-calculus is an instance of it, as the computation of a recursor on a constructor is named $`(\iota)`$ after the rule of the inductive types.
- One erasure, parametrized by the set of grades it erases, rather than an erasure for the run time and another for the proofs, each with clauses of its own: each kind of type declaration gives the clauses of its terms once, and the erasure of each mode is an instance.
- The erasures by two sets are compared by an order on the target language, in which a term is less defined than another when it has $`\text{↯}`$ in place of some of its parts, or a skeleton in place of the term of a recursor, rather than by erasing an erased term again, which would need grades in the target language.
- A constant keeps the grades of its instance, rather than having one copy that receives its erased parts at run time: the parts of its body that the erasure removes depend on these grades, so that each instance has a program of its own, with nothing left of the grades.

### Choices on the Meaning

The meaning is settled by the following choices:

- The meaning is given by the computation of the terms, as in the meaning explanations of Martin-Löf type theory, rather than by a model in another theory, such as sets: a judgment says what its terms compute by the reduction of the typing, so that what it guarantees is about the terms of Sic themselves.
- The constant declarations are interpreted, rather than given a meaning of their own: an axiom or a foreign constant has no body to compute, and the soundness holds under every interpretation that gives each of them a term of its type, so that a program depends on its declarations as on hypotheses.
- Every judgment of the typing has a meaning, the telescopes in a sort and the arguments of an instance included, rather than the closed equality alone, so that the soundness is stated for each judgment that the rules derive, and the application of a second-order variable has the meaning of its arguments.
- The terms of a proposition need not compute: the equality of a proposition relates the terms themselves, all when it is true and none when it is false, so that a proof, which the erasure removes, is never computed, and the meaning is exact for the rule of the irrelevance of the proofs.
- A type of a sort is given alone, with the equality of its terms, and two types are equal when they have the same equality of terms and the same relations of run time, rather than by a relation between two types that compares them clause by clause: the fields of a constructor form a telescope, whose types depend on the fields before them, and the partial equivalence relations then follow by induction on one type, with the condition that equal fields give the rest of the telescope the same equality, as [Abel, Öhman and Vezzosi](https://doi.org/10.1145/3158111) require of the families of types; the price is that the meaning equates types that the typing does not, such as two data types without constructors, which leaves the soundness unchanged.
- Two corecursors are equal when their observations at equal arguments are equal, rather than when their branches are, at equal arguments and states: the equality then reads what a term of a codata type gives, not how it is built, as the partial equivalence relations of the types of functions relate the functions by their applications in [Abel, Öhman and Vezzosi](https://doi.org/10.1145/3158111), and it holds unchanged for the corecursors whose branches pass on states, which the recursive blocks give; the observations compute once the reduction steps on them.
- Each corecursor is observed at the instance it carries, rather than at the instance of the type whose equality is given: the reduction of an observation ignores the instance, as that of a recursor does, and the equality of the terms of a type then depends on its levels only through their values, as for the data types, whose constructors are compared whatever the instance they carry.
- The destructors of a codata type are a telescope of the second order, whose equality relates families of abstractions, one for each destructor, rather than the terms of the type themselves: the meaning of the type then depends on its declaration alone, the type of a destructor that depends on the observations before it reads them as the values of second-order variables, and the condition that equal abstractions give the destructors after them the same equality is the one of the telescopes.
- The equality of the instances of a recursive block is the least relation closed under the clauses of its constructors, defined as the relation that every closed relation contains, as in the fixed point theorem of [Tarski](https://doi.org/10.2140/pjm.1955.5.285), rather than by an inductive definition of the metalanguage beside the graph: the same definition gives the greatest relations of the recursive codata types and composes through the nesting of a block in a type declared before it, whose meaning then relates the fields of the type of the block by the relation of the block, while an inductive definition of the metalanguage would have to be nested in the graph of the sorts, at an index rather than at a parameter.
- The meaning of the destructors of a block is stated at two closed substitutions of their scope, as that of the fields at two arguments of the parameters, rather than in a context extended by each destructor: the destructors after a recursive destructor do not depend on it, which the meaning states by quantifying over every abstraction for it, while a context extended by it would give it the meaning of the instance of the block, which the meaning of the block defines.
- Each entry is meaningful in the whole signature, rather than in the signature before it: a data type quantifies over the closed terms of its fields, which a larger signature may add, so that the meaning of an entry in the signature before it would not carry to the whole signature, where the entry is used.
- The closed judgments require the names of their signature to be distinct, which makes the computation deterministic, rather than the signature to be meaningful, which they do not use.
- What the usage and the ownership guarantee at run time, and the soundness of the erasure, wait for the calling convention, which a later step of this RFC fixes, and are then stated for this convention only, rather than for every evaluation order, a generality that no program of Sic would use.

### Choices on the Run Time

The relation of run time is settled by the following choices:

- The relation of run time is a graph of its own, defined in the meaning at each level together with the equality of the terms and reading it, as the relation of [Abel, Danielsson and Eriksson](https://github.com/graded-type-theory/graded-type-theory/blob/1f720ee636cb16aec130cc1ddc1c32acea8809d0/Graded/Erasure/LogicalRelation.agda) is defined on their reducible types, rather than a part of the equality of the terms: the equality of the terms of a type knows nothing of the erasure, and only the relation of run time reads the grades of the type.
- The equality of two types compares their relations of run time, rather than their equalities of terms alone: two instances of a type at grades that erase a field in one and keep it in the other have the same equality of terms but not the same relation of run time, so that a meaning that equated them would let the rule of conversion give a term a relation of run time that its erasure does not have; the price is that the equality of the terms of a universe reads the relations of run time of the universe below it, whose uniqueness its transitivity needs.
- The equality of the terms of a universe gives two types a common relation of run time in each program, while the equality of two closed types states that they have the same relations of run time, rather than a common one as the universe does: a universe thus gives each of its types a relation of run time, which every type that the typing gives has, since it is a term of a universe or of a universe of propositions, while the equality of the closed types needs no relation of run time of its own, since the graph of the equality of a type gives none.
- The rest of a telescope of fields, and the destructors after a destructor, are of run time for each term that the meaning gives to the field or to the destructor, rather than for every term or for the terms related at run time: a field erased at run time says nothing at run time of its term, and the types after it depend on this term all the same, so that only the meaning tells which terms they are given at.
- A term of an instance of a data type is related to a constructor in the heap where it is related, and a term of an instance of a codata type is related in each heap that extends it, rather than both in the same way: a constructor is read at its address, which every later heap keeps, while the observation of a value of a codata type runs later, at arguments that may be computed after the value.
- The terms of a universe and those of a proposition are related to every address, rather than to $`\text{↯}`$: the erasure removes the types, and the entries whose type is a proposition have a grade without use at run time, so that nothing at run time reads these values.
- A proof is related to every address without computing, whether it is a field or a term of its own, rather than once it computes, as the types are: the meaning of a proposition tells only that its proofs are equal, and never that they compute, so that the fundamental lemma could relate neither a proof, which it cannot show to compute, nor a substitution that gives a variable of a proposition a field of a constructor.
- The grades that decide whether a field or a destructor is related at run time are those of the instance of the type, rather than those that the term carries: the typing gives a constructor the instance of its type, and the equality of two instances keeps their grades.
- The relation is written with $`\circledR`$, after the relation of Abel, Danielsson and Eriksson, rather than with $`\mathsf{run}`$, which already names the run-time multiplicity of a grade.

### One RFC

The type system was first split into four RFCs, the grades, the core, the data and codata types, and the observational equality, linked by a contract that each kind of type declaration fulfills.
The split did not hold: the data and codata types kept deferring to the equality what they needed, the indexed families, the extensionality of the recursive types, and the liftings through some nested types, while the equality is defined on the data and codata types.
One RFC with short annexes keeps the definitions readable, one subject at a time, without a cycle between documents.

## Prior Art

The type system builds on the following work:

- The abstract interpretation of [Cousot and Cousot](https://doi.org/10.1145/512950.512973) and its [best abstractions](https://doi.org/10.1145/567752.567778) give the meaning of the multiplicities and of the grades.
- Grades on binders come from [McBride](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-30936-1_12) and the quantitative type theory of [Atkey](https://doi.org/10.1145/3209108.3209189), which [Idris 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.00480) implements with the multiplicities $`0`$, $`1`$ and $`\omega`$, and threads its effects through a linear world token, and [Linear Haskell](https://ghc.gitlab.haskell.org/ghc/doc/users_guide/exts/linear_types.html) with $`1`$ and $`\omega`$.
- The graded dependent type theory of [Abel, Danielsson and Eriksson](https://doi.org/10.1145/3607862), extended in their [journal version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29716) and [formalized in Agda](https://github.com/graded-type-theory/graded-type-theory), on which the rules of the type system are built: it parametrizes its rules by a modality and a mode structure, which the grades and the modes instantiate, and [Eriksson, Abel and Danielsson](https://doi.org/10.1145/3828677) add the well-behaved greatest lower bounds that the usage of recursion needs.
- The [graded modal dependent type theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.13163) of Moon, Eades and Orchard carries grades in typing, and [Granule](https://doi.org/10.1145/3341714) is a language built on graded modal types, whose semirings include exact counts.
- The levels follow those of [Lean](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/The-Type-System/Universes/), built from zero, the successor, the maximum and prenex variables, and the sort variables the [sort polymorphism of Rocq](https://rocq-prover.org/doc/master/refman/addendum/universe-polymorphism.html), whose theory [Bounded Sort Polymorphism with Elimination Constraints](https://doi.org/10.1145/3776732) gives.
- The second-order variables follow the contextual types of [Nanevski, Pfenning and Pientka](https://doi.org/10.1145/1352582.1352591), whose metavariables stand for terms under the variables of a context, and whose substitution of the metavariables is defined apart from that of the variables.
- The judgments are those of Martin-Löf type theory, and their meaning follows its meaning explanations, in which a judgment is true when its terms compute as it says, as [Nordström, Petersson and Smith](https://intuitionistic.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2010/07/nordstrom-et-al-type-theory.pdf) present them, and the signature follows the global environment of [Rocq](https://rocq-prover.org/doc/master/refman/language/cic.html), an ordered list of declarations, definitions and assumptions.
- The uniqueness types of [Clean](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0960129500070109), from Barendsen and Smetsers, guarantee that a value has no other reference, so that it can be updated in place; [Marshall, Vollmer and Orchard](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-99336-8_13) relate uniqueness, a guarantee on the past uses, to linearity, a restriction of the future ones, [Marshall and Orchard](https://doi.org/10.1145/3649848) add fractions to share a unique value for a while, and [Lorenzen, White, Dolan, Eisenberg and Lindley](https://doi.org/10.1145/3674642) give OCaml a uniqueness mode on its types; all of them check uniqueness in the typing, where Sic checks the ownership by a judgment of its own, after the usage of Abel, Danielsson and Eriksson, which they check apart from the typing, and with the grades of the usage.
- The strict propositions follow the `Prop` of [Agda](https://agda.readthedocs.io/en/latest/language/prop.html) and the `SProp` of Rocq, both from [Gilbert, Cockx, Sozeau and Tabareau](https://doi.org/10.1145/3290316).
- The weak head reduction follows the decision procedure for the equality of [Abel, Öhman and Vezzosi](https://doi.org/10.1145/3158111), and the erasure the extraction of Abel, Danielsson and Eriksson, which it extends with a set of erased grades; the order between erasures follows the approximants of the Böhm trees of [Kerinec, Manzonetto and Pagani](https://doi.org/10.23638/LMCS-16(3:6)2020), below which the undefined value lies.
- [Verus](https://doi.org/10.1145/3586037) separates the code that runs from the specifications and proofs by [modes of variables](https://verus-lang.github.io/verus/guide/reference-var-modes.html), as the modes of the constants of Sic, and tracks ghost resources linearly, which the grades `none / linear` and `linear / unrestricted` express in Sic.

## Unresolved Questions

These questions remain open:

- The annexes are written as the system is settled, subject by subject, so that the list above grows with them.
- What do the run-time multiplicities $`1`$ and $`\omega`$ mean for a program that runs?
  The meaning of the grades counts the uses that a term makes, and the uses made when it runs depend on the evaluation order, which a later step of this RFC fixes.
- What do the usage and the ownership of a term guarantee of its erasure when it runs, and is the erasure sound for the meaning?
  The statements need the evaluation order, which a later step of this RFC fixes, with the calling convention.
- Does the type system embrace duality all the way, and which statements would show it?
  Three statements together would answer, once the annexes define the data and codata types:
  - The rules are closed under an involution that exchanges each construct with its dual, the data and the codata types, their introductions and their eliminations, the recursor and the corecursor: the dual of every rule is a rule of the system.
  - The transposition of a block of declared types in a program of the core, which turns data types into codata types by refunctionalization and back by defunctionalization, preserves the typing, the grades, the erasure and the reduction, and its two directions are mutual inverses, as [Binder, Jabs, Skupin and Ostermann](https://doi.org/10.1145/3371098) show for a language without dependent types, after [Rendel, Trieflinger and Ostermann](https://doi.org/10.1145/2784731.2784763), who proposed the two transformations as a test of the symmetry of data and codata.
  - The asymmetries that remain are listed, each justified by a limit of the logic rather than by a choice, since the full duality, as that of [Wadler](https://doi.org/10.1145/944705.944723) between call-by-value and call-by-name, needs a classical sequent calculus.

  Four obstacles to the transposition are already known:
  - Strict positivity: a recursion whose motive takes a term of the transposed type as an argument, as the addition of natural numbers, becomes a destructor that takes a term of its own type, which strict positivity forbids to keep the system consistent; [Binder, Skupin, Süberkrüb and Ostermann](https://doi.org/10.1145/3649846) avoid it by working in an inconsistent type theory, so that the transposition in Sic can only be partial, on a domain to characterize.
  - Dependent elimination: the motive of the recursor depends on the term it eliminates, while the result of a destructor depends on the earlier observations of the term only; the dual would be destructors whose results depend on the observed term, as the self parameters of [Binder, Skupin, Süberkrüb and Ostermann](https://doi.org/10.1145/3649846).
  - Anonymous eliminations: the recursor and the corecursor are terms without names, so that an exact round trip needs either labels on them that tell a local elimination from a global one, as the local and global names of [Binder, Jabs, Skupin and Ostermann](https://doi.org/10.1145/3371098), or a statement of the inverse up to the lifting of the eliminations into definitions.
  - The order of the signature: the transposed block lists every elimination of the original block in the program, whose motives may mention entries declared after the block, while an entry of a signature refers only to the entries before it.
- Should a block mix data types and codata types?
  A block is a block of data types or a block of codata types, never both, so that the types that are inductive in some positions and coinductive in others are declared by nesting a block of one kind in a block of the other, declared before it, which fixes the order of their fixed points, as the mixed types of [Basold and Geuvers](https://doi.org/10.1145/2933575.2934514) do.
  A mixed block would let a data type and a codata type refer to each other in one declaration, at the price of a meaning for least and greatest fixed points taken together, without an order between them, and of rules of formation, of usage and of ownership for a block whose types are known some by their constructors and some by their destructors; whether the nesting covers every need, or only reorders the declarations of a program, is to be settled with the recursive blocks.
